ownsi proves that a domain belongs to you. You add one TXT record, we read DNS, and you get one of
three answers — proved, not yet, or here is exactly what is wrong with the record you created.

That third answer is the product. Most verification flows tell you "not verified" and leave you to
guess. ownsi names the reason: the panel appended your domain to the host, the value came back with
quotes around it, the resolvers are still holding a cached "does not exist" for four more minutes.

## The two surfaces

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Read a zone" icon="globe" href="/concepts/zone-reading">
    Public and unauthenticated. Give it a name and it streams what DNS says: who the nameservers
    are, which provider they belong to, and how long that provider usually takes to publish.
    Nothing is written, and no account is needed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Claim a domain" icon="key" href="/concepts/challenge-record">
    Belongs to an account. Issues a token, tells you the record to create, and keeps checking until
    it finds it. The token is stable for the life of the claim.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What you can rely on

<Steps>
  <Step title="A failed lookup never carries records">
    Outcomes are tagged unions. A DNS answer is either `{ type: "answered", records }` or
    `{ type: "failed", reason }` — there is no shape where you can read records off a lookup that
    did not happen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Errors carry a stable code">
    Every failure is `{ error: { code, message, docsUrl } }`. Branch on `code`; the `message` is
    written for a person and may be reworded. `docsUrl` links to [the entry on this
    site](/errors).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The token survives the lifecycle">
    Archiving, restoring and rechecking all preserve it. A record still in the zone verifies
    without the owner reopening their DNS panel.
  </Step>
</Steps>
