ownsi proves that you control a domain's DNS. You add one TXT record, we read DNS from three public
resolvers, and you get one of three answers — proved, not yet, or here is exactly what is wrong with
the record you created.

That third answer is the product. Most verification flows tell you "not verified" and leave you to
guess. ownsi names the reason: the panel appended your domain to the host, the value came back with
quotes around it, the resolvers are still holding a cached "does not exist" for four more minutes.

## What you get

A dated attestation: *"on 15 June, this account demonstrated control of `acme.com`'s DNS zone."*

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="It is about a moment" icon="clock">
    A fact about the day it was granted, not a claim about today. That is what makes it something
    you can show someone.
  </Card>
  <Card title="It never decays" icon="infinity">
    Once granted it is never revoked and never re-checked. Delete the TXT record afterwards if you
    like — the proof does not depend on it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Nothing is locked behind it" icon="unlock">
    No feature switches on. The attestation is the product, and you decide who sees it.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The three answers

Every check ends in exactly one of these, and the third is not a worse version of the second.

| Answer | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Proved** | The token is published and the resolvers agree. | Nothing. [Share it](/concepts/sharing-a-proof) if you want to. |
| **Not there yet** | Nothing is wrong. DNS has not caught up. | Wait. ownsi tells you roughly how long. |
| **Something is wrong** | The record exists but not the way it needs to. | One named [reason and one fix](/diagnostics/catalogue). |

The gap between the second and the third is the whole point. Telling someone to fix a record that
is already correct is the failure this product exists to avoid.

<Note>
  ownsi has an API, and it is not open yet. The routes that belong to an account answer only the
  app's own session, and there are no public keys. It is [documented in
  full](/api-reference/introduction) anyway, because the shape it will have when it opens is the
  shape it has now.
</Note>
