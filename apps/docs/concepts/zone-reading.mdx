Before you sign in — before ownsi knows who you are — it can already tell you who answers for your
domain, which provider that is, and how long an edit there usually takes to reach the internet.

Nothing is written, nothing is created, and no account is involved.

## Why it happens first

Because it changes what the next screen can say. Instead of *"add a TXT record somewhere"*, it can
say *"in Cloudflare, go to DNS → Records, and this usually publishes in about a minute"* — your
panel's own field names, and a number that came from your own zone.

It is also the honest answer to the question anyone has before trusting a new tool with anything:
*do you actually know my DNS?*

### The two waits, and why both are shown

| | |
|---|---|
| **Your provider publishing** | *When will my edit leave the panel?* How long that provider is known to take to push a change to its own nameservers. |
| **Resolvers forgetting** | *When will the rest of the internet stop saying this name does not exist?* Read from your own zone, and usually the longer of the two. |

The second one is the wait people mistake for a broken record. You can see the record in your panel,
you can even see it with `dig` against your own nameservers, and the rest of the world still says it
is not there — because it is repeating an answer it cached before you created it.

## It tells you what it changed about your input

Type it however you have it: `HTTPS://WWW.Acme.com/pricing`, a trailing dot, capitals, an
internationalised name. ownsi normalises it to `acme.com` and **says so**, rather than quietly
reading a different name than the one you typed.

If you type something nobody can own — `co.uk`, `github.io` — it says that too, and suggests the
name in front of it.

## When it cannot answer

Three different problems, and they are worth telling apart:

| What happened | What it means |
|---|---|
| Not a domain name | The text is not something DNS can be asked about. Nothing to do with your DNS. |
| No nameservers at all | Usually the domain is not registered, or it was registered minutes ago and the registry has not published the delegation yet. |
| ownsi could not reach DNS | Ours, not yours. Worth trying again in a moment. |

That last one is never dressed up as a problem with your domain. [ownsi keeps the three
apart](/concepts/verification) everywhere, not only here.

## What happens to it afterwards

The answer is stored for a short while, so asking about the same name twice in a row does not send
two queries out to the internet. A public endpoint that queried DNS on every request would be an
open resolver, which is not a thing worth being.

Building against this? [The zone read streams its two
frames](/api-reference/streaming), and it is the one route that needs no account.
